The Bet Multiplier — the One Decision That Sets Your Pace

The bet multiplier is the lever most players never touch — they leave it wherever it was and let it quietly decide how fast they progress. It should be the first thing you think about each session, because it changes what your dice are worth depending on what you are trying to do.

What the Bet Multiplier Actually Does

Mechanics

Raising your bet costs more dice per roll and pays more coins per roll. That sounds like it scales everything equally — but it does not, and that gap is the whole strategy.

Scales ↑

Coins — these multiply with the bet

Each roll's coin payout grows with your bet, so a high bet is the fastest way to fund an expensive castle upgrade. For pure coin-collecting, higher is genuinely better (within what your dice stack can sustain).

Flat

Triggers — these do NOT scale, only roll

Cards, chests, event milestones and attack/defence symbols fire per roll, at the same odds each time. Raising your bet does not raise the chance per roll — it just means your stack buys fewer rolls. So for anything that depends on trigger chances, a low bet gets you more chances and finishes the job faster.

💡 The mental model: bet high when you want bigger payouts, bet low when you want more rolls. You can rarely have both — pick which one your goal needs.

When to Bet High vs Low

Decision

Match the bet to what you are actually after right now, and switch deliberately rather than leaving it pinned.

🔺 Bet HIGH when…

  • You are short a few big coins to finish a castle and advance dreamlands.
  • A coin-boost event is live and multiplies every payout — stack the event bonus on top of a high bet.
  • You want to clear a build quickly and have a dice reserve you can afford to drain fast.

🔻 Bet LOW when…

  • You are hunting cards to complete a set — more rolls, more card drops.
  • An event rewards number of triggers or attacks rather than raw coins.
  • Your dice stack is small and you want it to last long enough to hit something.
  • You are just topping up coins during a quiet, unboosted stretch.

The Auto-Bet Trap

Watch For

Auto-roll is the fastest way to drain a stack without noticing. It is genuinely useful when you are watching a high-bet coin session — but it is dangerous when left running at a high multiplier while your real goal is cards or event triggers, because it will eat three to ten dice per roll and close your session before you've had enough rolls to trigger anything.

Use auto for coins, not cards

Auto-roll at a high bet to fund a build is fine. Auto-roll at a high bet while waiting for a rare card is the worst of both worlds — you burn the stack and still get few chances.

Cap it before you start

Decide the roll count or dice amount before you hit auto, so it stops on its own instead of running to zero. The calculator shows how many rolls a stack buys at each bet.

A Sensible Default If You Hate Micromanaging

Shortcut

If switching bets every session feels like a chore, leave it at a mid-range multiplier (roughly 10–15) as your everyday setting. It earns coins at a respectable rate while still buying enough rolls that cards and triggers keep landing — it never maximises either goal, but it is close to optimal for "a bit of everything" play. Then make only two deliberate exceptions: spike up for a final castle push during a coin-boost window, and drop to 1–3 for a focused card-hunt when a set is one or two rare cards from closing. Two switches a week is most of the available value.
