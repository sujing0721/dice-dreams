Dice Planner & Bet Multiplier Calculator

Two quick tools that turn the strategy into numbers for your exact situation. Split a stack of dice across your goals, then check whether your current bet multiplier actually fits what you are hunting for. Both run entirely in your browser — nothing you enter is sent anywhere.

How the maths works: coins scale with your bet, but card, chest and event triggers fire once per roll and do not scale. That is the trade-off these tools quantify. The full reasoning is on the bet multiplier guide.

How to Read Your Results

Worked Example

The numbers are only useful if you know which way to lean. Here is how a real stack gets planned, step by step, so you can copy the method onto your own totals.

Step 1

Anchor the split to what is live this week

Open the planner and check the app first: is a tournament or race running or about to start? If a big event goes live in the next day or two, drag Event reserve to 60% or higher — a stack spent on an event that pays dice back at milestone tiers effectively refunds part of itself. If the week is quiet with nothing boosted, move that slice into Castle building instead, because steady progression is never wasted and events rotate back around constantly.

Step 2

Work a 500-dice example

Suppose you hold 500 dice mid-week with a card-collection event opening tomorrow. Set Event reserve to 60% (300 dice for the event), Castle building to 20% (100 dice to keep a dreamland finish moving), and Cards/attacks to 20% (100 dice). That split gives the event the bulk while you still make progress on the two slower goals. After the event closes, re-run it: the refunds and milestone dice you just earned are your new total, and a quiet week means you drag Building back up to 40–50%.

Step 3

Read the bet trade-off honestly

Look at Rolls you will get versus Trigger chances together, not just coins per roll. A bet multiplier of 30 on 300 dice gives you only about 10 rolls — roughly ten chances at a card, chest, or raid trigger — while a bet of 5 on the same 300 dice gives you 60 rolls for six times the trigger chances. High bets win the coin race; low bets win the card and event-token race. Neither is "better"; they serve different goals.

Step 4

Match the bet to what you are actually hunting

Use the verdict box. If it tells you your stack is thin for the bet you picked, believe it: the fastest way to feel stuck is to run a 25–50 bet on a three-digit stack and burn out before a trigger lands. The comfortable default for most players is a mid-range bet (around 10–15) that keeps rolls plentiful while still paying meaningful coins — then drop to 1–3 deliberately for a short card-hunting burst when a set is almost complete.

Sensitivity check: if moving a slider changes the verdict text, that is a signal you are near a threshold worth pausing on. The tools are a sanity check on a decision you were going to make anyway — they stop you from draining a stack at the wrong multiplier or dumping event dice on a build.
